## Variant A — the key (handing over power, literally)
I handed you the key —
my door, my lock, my name
on the lease. You turned it.
The door swung wide.
And I stood in the hall
holding nothing, not even
the small weight of the key.

## Variant B — the asking (power is the ask, not the answer)
Before the yes, there was the asking —
my voice, unsteady, in the quiet room.
The answer came easy after that.
But the power was never the yes.
It was the second I chose
to open my mouth at all.

## Variant C — the rain errand (delegation's hidden cost)
You ran my errand in the rain
while I stayed dry behind the glass.
The parcel came. The list was ticked.
Everything was done — except the walking,
the wet sleeves, the small proud
tiredness of having gone myself.

("behind the glass" replaced "at the window" in a second edit: the glass
makes the separation from the doing tactile, which is the poem's point.)

## Pick: Variant C
C wins on the judge's own order. (1) Subject: it is exactly the agency that
passes between two actors when one acts for the other — the brief's own
example. (2) It moves: rain, wet sleeves, a window — a scene you can feel,
where A is a clean metaphor but a familiar one (handing over a key) and B is
mostly an idea in a quiet room. (3) Insight: delegation doesn't just save
effort, it takes the small proud tiredness of having gone — a true thing
about agency nobody had quite put into words, and the final line lands it.
(4) Craft: every word earns its place, no slogan. (5) Freshness: no other
entry will have this scene. A is the runner-up; B is the weakest (abstract,
nearest to a lecture).
